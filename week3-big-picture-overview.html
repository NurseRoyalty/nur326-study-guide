Big Picture Overview

This page doesn't teach anything new — it's a map of Week 3's four topics, so you can see how they fit together instead of studying each one like it's unrelated to the rest. Two of the four pages form a direct progression (the urinary tract, healthy through failure); the third bridges back into that same system through one complication; the fourth is its own separate sensory-systems topic. This page shows where each idea is actually taught in full, and where it just comes back as supporting detail. No flashcards or quiz questions here — just the map. (The Comprehensive Study Guide, Topic 1, is different — it re-teaches everything in full; this page only summarizes.)

Three Clusters, Not Four Separate Topics

ClusterTopicsThe Common Thread
The Urinary Tract, Healthy to FailingRenal Disorders → Acute Kidney Injury & Chronic Kidney DiseaseThese two build on each other directly: Renal Disorders covers infection, obstruction, stones, cancer, and glomerular disease at the organ level, while AKI & CKD covers what happens when the kidney's overall filtering function itself fails — acutely or over time. Nearly every CKD manifestation traces back to one of the kidneys' five key jobs, taught once on the second page.
Bridging Back Into the Urinary TractMale Reproductive DisordersThree of its four conditions (testicular cancer, prostate cancer, erectile dysfunction) are self-contained. But BPH's obstructive-nephropathy complication is the same by-level obstruction mechanism taught on Renal Disorders — the enlarged prostate is simply one more possible cause of a bladder/urethra-level blockage.
A Separate Sensory SystemVisual & Sensory DisordersNot connected to the urinary/reproductive topics mechanically — it's grouped into Week 3 as its own sensory-systems unit. Internally, though, it has the same "sudden and painless is still an emergency" thread running through retinal detachment and (sudden and painful) acute closed-angle glaucoma.

If you're not sure where to start a review pass, work through Renal Disorders before AKI & CKD — the second page assumes you already know how infection, obstruction, stones, and glomerular disease can independently damage the kidney — then treat Male Reproductive and Visual & Sensory as self-contained units.

The Four Topics at a Glance

#TopicOne-Line Takeaway
1Renal DisordersFive conditions at the organ level: pyelonephritis (an ascending or bloodstream kidney infection that can progress to urosepsis), urinary obstruction (by level, speed, and internal-vs-external cause), nephrolithiasis (four stone types, calcium most common), urologic cancers (kidney and bladder), and glomerular disease (glomerulonephritis, nephrotic syndrome, glomerulopathy).
2Acute Kidney Injury & Chronic Kidney DiseaseAKI is classified by where the problem sits relative to the kidney (prerenal, intrarenal, postrenal) and is usually reversible. CKD is staged by GFR, driven by a self-perpetuating proteinuria/angiotensin-II cycle, and is not reversible — nearly every manifestation and every drug used traces back to one of the kidneys' five normal jobs failing.
3Male Reproductive DisordersTesticular cancer (young men, painless mass, high survival), BPH (benign prostate overgrowth causing obstructive urinary symptoms), prostate cancer (usually silent until metastatic, controversial PSA screening), and erectile dysfunction (organic vs. psychogenic, nitric oxide/cGMP physiology, the nitrate danger).
4Visual & Sensory DisordersSix conditions sorted by speed and pain: gradual/painless (cataracts, retinopathy, macular degeneration, open-angle glaucoma) versus sudden (retinal detachment — painless but urgent; closed-angle glaucoma — painful and urgent), plus Meniere's disease (episodic vertigo/hearing loss/tinnitus).

High-Alert Safety Rules — All in One Place

Every danger callout across the four pages, condensed to a phrase. This is meant for a quick scan, not a replacement for the full explanation — click through to a topic for the details behind any rule.

TopicThe Hard Rules
Renal DisordersUrosepsis — a UTI (upper or lower) can progress to sepsis in elderly or already-sick patients. BCG intravesical therapy is contraindicated in immunocompromised patients — it's a live attenuated organism and can cause disseminated infection.
AKI & CKDErythropoietin's black-box warning — overcorrecting hemoglobin above 10 g/dL raises stroke/MI/thromboembolism risk. Opioids are retained far longer than normal in severe CKD, raising respiratory depression risk.
Male Reproductive DisordersPDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil) can never be combined with nitrates — both drop blood pressure through the same pathway. Priapism (an erection lasting more than 4 hours) is a true emergency that can cause permanent damage if untreated.
Visual & Sensory DisordersRetinal detachment is a sudden, painless emergency — the "curtain" description should raise immediate suspicion. Acute closed-angle glaucoma is a sudden, painful emergency that can cause permanent vision loss within hours.

Patterns you'll notice across these rules

  • "Sudden and painless is still an emergency." Retinal detachment causes no pain at all, and AKI's oliguria can be easy to write off as "just a little dehydrated" — neither presentation's calmness means the situation is calm.
  • "Trace every finding back to a job that stopped happening." The kidneys' five jobs work the same way the liver's five jobs do elsewhere in this course: pick the abnormal lab or symptom, ask which specific job is responsible, and the "why" falls out on its own.
  • "One blockage causes disease from two different directions." A kidney stone and an enlarged prostate are mechanically the same problem — a blockage backing pressure up the urinary tract — just at different levels and from different organs.
  • "Combining two drugs that do the same thing through the same pathway is the danger, not either drug alone." PDE5 inhibitors and nitrates both lower blood pressure via nitric oxide/cGMP; ACE inhibitors and ARBs (CKD) are never combined for the same reason.
  • "Screening isn't automatically good." PSA screening for prostate cancer is the clearest example this week — it can catch real cancer early, but it can also drive biopsies and treatment for disease that may never have caused harm.
