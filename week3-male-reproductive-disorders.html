Male Reproductive Disorders

This page covers four male reproductive conditions in the order the lecture teaches them: testicular cancer, benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), prostate cancer, and erectile dysfunction. The two prostate conditions are presented back to back because the lecture compares and contrasts them directly.

Testicular Cancer

TermDetail
How CommonRare overall — about 1% of all cancers in men. But it is the most common solid tumor in young men, typically diagnosed between ages 15 and 34.
Biggest Risk FactorCryptorchidism (an undescended testicle) — even if surgically corrected in childhood, the risk stays elevated for life.
Tumor OriginThe vast majority arise from germ cells (the cells that would normally become sperm) inside the testicle.
PresentationA painless mass or lump in the testicle is the classic finding — patients often find it themselves. Because it's painless, it's easy to ignore or delay reporting.
PrognosisSurvival rates are very high, even in later-stage disease, because testicular cancer responds well to treatment — this is a reassurance point the lecture makes explicitly.

Seminoma

  • A germ cell tumor type.
  • Grows more slowly.
  • Generally more radiosensitive and has a somewhat more favorable prognosis than nonseminoma.

Nonseminoma

  • The other germ cell tumor type.
  • Tends to grow and spread more quickly than seminoma.
  • Still highly treatable — the overall high-survival point still applies.
Trick: think "seminoma is the mellow one" — seminoma grows slower and stays more contained, while nonseminoma is the faster, more aggressive of the two germ cell types.

Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH)

TermDetail
Prostate's JobA gland that sits below the bladder and surrounds the urethra; it contributes fluid to semen.
What BPH IsA non-malignant (benign) overgrowth of the prostate's epithelial and smooth muscle tissue. It is not cancer and does not turn into cancer.
Proposed MechanismsTwo theories the lecture presents: a hormone imbalance (shifting androgen/estrogen ratio with age) and accumulation of DHT (dihydrotestosterone) in prostate tissue, which drives cell overgrowth.
SymptomsBecause the enlarged prostate squeezes the urethra, symptoms mimic a lower urinary tract problem: hesitancy, weak stream, frequency, urgency, nocturia, and a feeling of incomplete emptying.
ComplicationObstructive nephropathy — if the obstruction is severe and prolonged enough, urine can back up all the way to the kidneys and cause kidney damage.

Obstruction backing pressure up to the kidney follows the same by-level logic covered on Renal Disorders' Urinary Obstruction section

ApproachHow It Works
Watchful WaitingFor mild symptoms — monitor rather than treat immediately.
5-Alpha Reductase InhibitorsFinasteride and dutasteride. Block the enzyme that converts testosterone to DHT, which shrinks the prostate over time. Effects take months to show.
Alpha-1 BlockersTamsulosin (Flomax) is the prototype. Relax smooth muscle in the prostate and bladder neck, working much faster than the 5-alpha reductase inhibitors — but they don't shrink the gland itself.
Combination TherapyUsing a 5-alpha reductase inhibitor and an alpha-1 blocker together for moderate-to-severe symptoms — one shrinks the gland long-term, the other relieves symptoms right away.
Trick to tell the two drug classes apart: "-osteride shrinks it, -osin relaxes it fast." Finasteride/dutasteride (-osteride) are the slow shrinkers; tamsulosin (-osin) is the fast relaxer.

Prostate Cancer

TermDetail
How CommonThe most common cancer diagnosed in men in the U.S.
Risk FactorsIncreasing age, race (higher incidence and mortality in Black men), and family history.
PresentationMostly asymptomatic in early stages — that's a key difference from BPH, which causes symptoms right away because of where it grows. Prostate cancer often isn't found until it has metastasized, classically to bone.
PSA ScreeningControversial. PSA (prostate-specific antigen) can be elevated by cancer, but also by BPH and prostatitis, so it isn't cancer-specific. Screening can catch cancer early, but it also drives overdiagnosis — biopsies and treatment for slow-growing cancers that may never have caused harm, exposing patients to unnecessary risk and side effects.
Gleason ScoreA grading system based on how abnormal prostate tissue looks under the microscope — higher scores mean more aggressive, less differentiated cancer.

Erectile Dysfunction (ED)

TermDetail
Primary (Organic) CausesA physical problem with blood flow, nerves, or hormones — vascular disease, diabetes, low testosterone, certain medications, pelvic surgery/injury.
Secondary (Psychogenic) CausesPsychological — performance anxiety, depression, relationship stress. Can occur even when the physical machinery is normal.
Normal Erection PhysiologySexual stimulation triggers nitric oxide (NO) release, which raises cyclic GMP (cGMP) inside penile smooth muscle. Rising cGMP relaxes that smooth muscle, allowing blood to flow in and fill the erectile tissue.
PDE5 InhibitorsSildenafil (Viagra) is the prototype. PDE5 (phosphodiesterase-5) is the enzyme that normally breaks down cGMP; blocking it lets cGMP stay elevated longer, sustaining the erection. Timing: taken before sexual activity, not a standing daily dose for most patients. Adverse effects: headache, flushing, visual changes.
Nitrate contraindication: PDE5 inhibitors must never be combined with nitrates (e.g., nitroglycerin). Both pathways lower blood pressure through the same nitric oxide/cGMP mechanism — combining them can cause a severe, life-threatening drop in blood pressure.
Priapism: a prolonged, painful erection unrelated to sexual stimulation, lasting more than 4 hours — a true urologic emergency. Untreated, it can cause permanent tissue damage and lasting erectile dysfunction. Call for immediate medical attention rather than waiting it out.

Self-Test Flashcards

Click a card to flip it and check your answer.

What is the single biggest risk factor for testicular cancer?
Cryptorchidism (an undescended testicle) — the risk stays elevated for life even after surgical correction.
What is the classic presentation of testicular cancer, and why is that concerning?
A painless mass or lump in the testicle — because it's painless, patients may delay reporting it.
How do seminoma and nonseminoma germ cell tumors differ?
Seminoma grows more slowly and is more radiosensitive; nonseminoma grows and spreads more quickly. Both have high overall survival.
What is BPH, and what two mechanisms are proposed to cause it?
A benign (non-cancerous) overgrowth of prostate epithelial and smooth muscle tissue, proposed to result from a hormone imbalance and/or DHT accumulation in prostate tissue.
Why do BPH symptoms mimic a lower urinary tract problem?
The enlarged prostate surrounds and squeezes the urethra, causing hesitancy, weak stream, frequency, urgency, nocturia, and incomplete emptying.
What's the difference between how 5-alpha reductase inhibitors and alpha-1 blockers treat BPH?
5-alpha reductase inhibitors (finasteride, dutasteride) block DHT production and shrink the prostate over months. Alpha-1 blockers (tamsulosin) relax prostate/bladder-neck smooth muscle and relieve symptoms fast, without shrinking the gland.
What serious complication can untreated, severe BPH lead to?
Obstructive nephropathy — urine backs up all the way to the kidneys and can cause kidney damage.
Why is prostate cancer often not caught early?
It's mostly asymptomatic in early stages — it's often not found until it has metastasized, classically to bone.
Why is PSA screening for prostate cancer controversial?
PSA isn't cancer-specific (BPH and prostatitis raise it too), and screening can lead to overdiagnosis — biopsies and treatment for slow-growing cancers that may never have caused harm.
What does a higher Gleason score mean?
More abnormal-looking, less differentiated prostate tissue under the microscope — a more aggressive cancer.
What is the normal physiologic pathway behind an erection, and how do PDE5 inhibitors work with it?
Nitric oxide raises cGMP, which relaxes penile smooth muscle and allows blood flow in. PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil) block the enzyme that breaks down cGMP, so it stays elevated longer and sustains the erection.
Why are PDE5 inhibitors dangerous with nitrates, and what is priapism?
Both lower blood pressure through the same nitric oxide/cGMP pathway — combining them can cause a severe, life-threatening blood pressure drop. Priapism is a painful erection lasting more than 4 hours unrelated to stimulation — a true emergency that can cause permanent damage if untreated.
