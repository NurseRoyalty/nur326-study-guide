Big Picture Overview

This page doesn't teach anything new — it's a map of Week 2's six topics, so you can see how they fit together instead of studying each one like it's unrelated to the rest. Three central concepts (hypoxemia from reduced oxygen delivery or poor gas exchange, inflammation as the driver of pathology, and infection as an acute trigger or chronic complication) keep resurfacing across all six topics. This page shows where these ideas are actually taught in full, and where they just come back as supporting detail. No flashcards or quiz questions here — just the map.

Four Clusters, Not Six Separate Topics

Six pages is a lot to hold in your head at once. All of them fall into one of four groups — knowing the group can matter more than memorizing all six titles.

ClusterTopicsThe Common Thread
Red Blood Cell ProblemsAnemia; PolycythemiaOxygen-carrying capacity gone wrong — too few RBCs or hemoglobin (anemia) or too many (polycythemia). Both cause tissue hypoxemia or hypercoagulability from different directions.
Airway ObstructionObstructive Airway Disorders (Asthma, COPD, Emphysema, Chronic Bronchitis)Airways are narrowed; air gets trapped on expiration; V/Q mismatch causes hypoxemia and hypercapnia. Inflammation and mucus production drive all three diseases, even though the underlying mechanisms differ.
Infection in the LungsPneumonia; Tuberculosis; URIAn infectious agent invades the lower or upper respiratory tract. All three cause inflammation, fluid accumulation, V/Q mismatch, and hypoxemia. TB is unique because it's chronic and requires long-term therapy; URI is unique because it's usually self-limited.
The Bridge Between Blood and LungsHow anemia affects oxygen delivery to tissues; how lung disease affects oxygen loading onto RBCsRBC problems (anemia) mean fewer oxygen-carrying vehicles; lung problems mean oxygen can't get INTO those vehicles. Both pathways lead to tissue hypoxemia.

If you're not sure where to start a review pass, working through the clusters in this order touches every recurring theme once before moving on, instead of jumping between unrelated systems.

The Six Topics at a Glance

#TopicOne-Line Takeaway
1AnemiaReduced RBC number or hemoglobin decreases oxygen-carrying capacity; types include iron/B12/folate deficiency, aplastic anemia, hemolytic anemia, and sickle cell disease.
2PolycythemiaToo many RBCs (opposite of anemia) causes blood thickening, increased viscosity, and thrombotic risk — either primary (polycythemia vera, a bone marrow cancer) or secondary (compensatory response to chronic hypoxemia).
3Obstructive Airway DisordersAsthma, chronic bronchitis, and emphysema all narrow airways and trap air on expiration; inflammation and V/Q mismatch drive hypoxemia and hypercapnia.
4PneumoniaBacterial, viral, or fungal infection in the alveoli causes fluid accumulation, inflammation, V/Q mismatch, and hypoxemia; severity depends on the organism and the patient's immune status.
5TuberculosisA chronic mycobacterial infection (Mycobacterium tuberculosis) that primarily affects the lungs; requires months of multi-drug therapy to prevent drug resistance.
6URI (Upper Respiratory Infection)Viral infection of the nose, sinuses, and throat; mostly self-limited, but can lead to complications like sinusitis, otitis media, or lower respiratory tract extension.

High-Alert Safety Rules — All in One Place

Every danger callout across the six pages, condensed to a phrase. This is meant for a quick scan, not a replacement for the full explanation — click through to a topic for the details behind any rule.

TopicThe Hard Rules
AnemiaSickle cell crisis is triggered by anything that raises oxygen demand or lowers oxygen — dehydration, stress, high altitude, fever, extreme temperatures. Vaso-occlusion causes severe pain and can be life-threatening. Recognize and treat immediately.
AnemiaIron dextran carries a fatal anaphylaxis risk from the dextran component — always give a test dose first with epinephrine immediately available. Never give without a test dose.
AnemiaESAs (epoetin alfa, darbepoetin) have a BLACK-BOX WARNING: never give if hemoglobin is GREATER than 10 g/dL — risk of stroke, MI, thromboembolism, and polycythemic state from overcorrection.
PolycythemiaPolycythemia vera patients are at high thrombotic risk from thick, viscous blood — monitor closely for stroke, MI, DVT/PE. Phlebotomy and anticoagulation are standard management.
Obstructive Airway DisordersStatus asthmaticus is a life-threatening emergency — a SILENT CHEST (no air movement) with CO2 >70 signals imminent respiratory failure. Prepare for intubation and IV epinephrine.
Obstructive Airway DisordersIn COPD patients, high-flow oxygen can suppress the respiratory drive — use lowest effective FiO2 and monitor closely. CO2 retention is the risk; titrate carefully.
PneumoniaCommunity-acquired pneumonia can progress rapidly to sepsis — recognize early signs (fever, tachycardia, tachypnea, hypotension) and start antibiotics and fluid resuscitation immediately.
PneumoniaAspiration pneumonia risk in patients with altered mental status, dysphagia, or recent anesthesia — NPO status, head-of-bed elevation, and careful feeding tube placement can prevent it.
TuberculosisTB requires LONG-TERM multi-drug therapy to prevent resistance — missed doses or early stopping allow resistant strains to emerge. Adherence is critical; directly observed therapy (DOT) is often used.
TuberculosisTB is reportable and requires contact investigation — close contacts need testing and possibly prophylactic therapy even if asymptomatic.
URIMost URIs are viral and don't respond to antibiotics — inappropriate antibiotic use drives resistance. Supportive care is standard; antibiotics only for suspected bacterial superinfection (sinusitis, otitis media with specific findings).

Patterns you'll notice across these rules

  • "Hypoxemia is the common endpoint — recognize it early." Whether from anemia (reduced O2 carriers), lung disease (poor gas exchange), or infection (inflammation/fluid), tissue hypoxemia is the threat. ABG/VBG and SpO2 monitoring matter across all six topics.
  • "Inflammation drives symptoms and complications." Asthma (bronchoconstriction), pneumonia (fluid), TB (tissue destruction), URI (nasal congestion), anemia (hemolysis), and polycythemia (viscosity) — inflammation is a thread connecting all six.
  • "Treatment can be as dangerous as the disease." High-flow oxygen suppresses respirations in COPD; ESAs overcorrect anemia to stroke risk; TB drugs have interactions; iron dextran causes anaphylaxis. Know the risk as well as the benefit.
  • "Adherence and follow-through save lives." TB therapy requires months; COPD patients need home oxygen and monitoring; anemia needs repeat labs to guide treatment adjustments. Short-term thinking (stopping TB drugs early, skipping doses) has long-term consequences.
  • "The 'silent' finding is often the most dangerous." A silent chest in asthma signals respiratory failure; silent hypoxemia in polycythemia precedes thrombosis; TB can be silent (latent) for years before reactivating.
